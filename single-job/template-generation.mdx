With a validated Success Profile in hand, Phase 2 designs the container before filling it. Rather than slotting content into a generic resume layout, the skill builds a role-specific template that determines section ordering, how your roles are presented, what titles best represent your work for this audience, and how many bullets each position deserves. None of your actual resume content is written yet — this phase is entirely about structure and strategy, ending in a checkpoint where you approve or adjust the blueprint before content matching begins.

## What template generation decides

Phase 2 makes four structural decisions, each grounded in the Success Profile from Phase 1:

### Section order and rationale

The skill determines which sections to include and in what order, based on the role archetype and what the Success Profile identifies as most important. A technical IC role might lead with a Skills section; a leadership role might lead with a Summary; a career-change resume might use a different ordering to direct attention strategically.

### Role consolidation

When your library contains multiple positions at the same company — or short-tenure roles with overlapping responsibilities — the skill evaluates whether to present them separately or as a single consolidated entry. The decision template makes both options explicit:

```
For {Company} with {N} positions:

OPTION A (Consolidated):
Title: "{Combined_Title}"
Dates: "{First_Start} - {Last_End}"
Rationale: {Why consolidation makes sense}

OPTION B (Separate):
Position 1: "{Title}" ({Dates})
Position 2: "{Title}" ({Dates})
Rationale: {Why separate makes sense}

RECOMMENDED: Option {A/B} because {reasoning}
```

### Title reframing options

For each role in the template, the skill proposes a primary title and alternatives, with rationale for each choice.

### Bullet allocation

The skill allocates a specific number of bullet slots to each role, weighted by recency and relevance to the target position. This allocation drives how many content matches Phase 3 needs to find for each role.

## Title Reframing

Your job titles were assigned by past employers using their internal terminology. That language may or may not resonate with the hiring team reviewing this application. Title reframing is the practice of presenting your role using terminology that better reflects what you actually did — as seen through the lens of your target audience.

The core rule is strict: **stay truthful to what you did, emphasize the aspect most relevant to the target role.** The company name and dates are never changed. The reframing only adjusts how the scope or focus of the role is described.

**Strategies the skill applies:**

1. **Emphasize different aspects** — "Graduate Researcher" → "Research Software Engineer" if the work was coding-heavy
2. **Use industry-standard terminology** — "Scientist III" → "Senior Research Scientist" for clearer seniority signaling
3. **Add specialization when truthful** — "Engineer" → "ML Engineer" if ML was substantial
4. **Adjust seniority indicators** — choosing between "Lead," "Senior," or "Staff" based on actual scope

**A real example:** A TPM transitioning to an ecology-sector PM role might see:
> `"Technical Program Manager"` → `"Program Manager, Environmental Systems"`

The reframing preserves the PM nature of the work while adding the domain language the hiring team uses. The skill proposes the reframing with its rationale and always offers alternatives — you approve the final choice at the checkpoint.

## Role Consolidation

**When to consolidate:**
- Same company, similar responsibilities across positions
- The target role values continuity over granular progression
- The combined narrative is stronger than the sum of its parts
- Page space is constrained and separate entries add little value

**When to keep separate:**
- Different companies (always separate — no exceptions)
- Dramatically different responsibilities where both sets matter independently
- The target role values a specific progression story
- One position has significantly more relevant experience and deserves prominence

The skill never makes a consolidation decision silently. Both options are presented with rationale, and the recommended path is flagged clearly. If the decision is genuinely ambiguous, the skill says so and asks for your preference directly.

## The template checkpoint

At the end of Phase 2, the skill presents the full template for your review:

```
Here's the optimized resume structure for this role:

STRUCTURE:
{Section order and rationale}

ROLE CONSOLIDATION:
{Decisions with options}

TITLE REFRAMING:
{Proposed titles with alternatives}

BULLET ALLOCATION:
Role 1: {N} bullets (most relevant)
Role 2: {N} bullets
...

Does this structure work? Any adjustments to:
- Role consolidation?
- Title reframing?
- Bullet allocation?
```

The skill waits for your approval before proceeding to Phase 2.5 (Experience Discovery) or Phase 3 (Content Matching). You can approve as-is, provide targeted feedback ("make the summary shorter," "lead with cloud experience"), or request a specific adjustment to consolidation or title choices.

<Tip>
If you have specific knowledge about the hiring team — for example, you've spoken with the recruiter, you know someone on the team, or you've read their engineering blog — this checkpoint is the ideal place to share it. Telling the skill "this team cares a lot about data-driven decision making" or "the hiring manager came from a startup background" will directly influence how your experience is emphasized in the template and in content matching.
</Tip>
